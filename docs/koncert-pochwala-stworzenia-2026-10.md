## §2 Programme — provisional, read off the Spotify running order

works.** The Pärt is cut into 01 [wstęp] … 09 [zakończenie] and threaded between every item —
it is the spine, not filler. (Structurally this is 9 Kart's braided Miserere again, in a new key:
there, one psalm distributed through eight works; here, one mirror returning nine times.)

- **Bach — CLOSED 11.09.2026: out.** Massenet's *Méditation de Thaïs* takes the solo slot
  instead (Radu's choice; see §8 for the objections that were raised and overruled). The note
  below is kept because it is the only written statement of *what the slot is for*, and that
  still governs if the solo is ever revisited. Original note: Florent cut him
  (07.09): the Bach was to be **solo violin, Radu Ropotan**, and a soloist playing alone breaks
  the Pärt–work–Pärt–work alternation. He is considering wrapping the solo in Pärt, or attaching
  it to a neighbouring item, and is putting Bach back in the playlist for a verdict.
  **My recommendation:** the violin is not a guest — *Spiegel im Spiegel* is a violin-and-piano
  piece, so if Radu plays the Pärt he is the one continuous voice of the evening (confirm the
  version; a cello *Spiegel* voids this whole argument). Then do **not** wrap the Bach in Pärt —
  let it **stand in the place where a Pärt return was due**. After eight returns the listener
  expects the mirror; instead the same instrument plays alone, unaccompanied. A form that never
  varies is a loop, and the nine returns are strong enough to survive — indeed be defined by —
  one absence. Placement: immediately before *Dona eis requiem*, because the Canticle strophe
  with **no work against it** is "quelli ke perdonano … et sostengo infirmitate et tribulatione".
  Decision rule for whether it goes in at all: does the Bach carry a strophe nothing else
  carries? If yes, in; if it is only "the soloist should have a solo", out. Length: keep it
  short and contemplative (Sarabanda BWV 1004, Largo BWV 1005, Andante BWV 1003) — **not** the
  Chaconne, the Lark already runs 15:50. Precedent: Radu played Bach/Enescu solo at Wołanie Gór.
- **Orff.** The Spotify release calls the set *"Concerto di voci"*; Orff's set is
  ***Concento di voci***. More importantly: *Laudes creaturarum* (1954) is written for
  **speaking chorus** (Sprechchor) with percussion, not for singers. If that holds, the centre of
  this evening is the one moment the choir stops singing and **speaks** St Francis' Umbrian —
  which is a dramaturgical and a poster fact. Confirm against the score being used.
- **Sykulski.** *Stoi lód na Prośnie* — Prosna is a river, capital P (this exact typo was fixed
  once already in `concerts.yaml`). It is a **wokaliza** with words added later, not an
  arrangement of a folk song.
- **Ešenvalds' *Stars*** is scored with tuned water glasses — a practical requirement, and a
  detail worth a `note` on the work.
- **Lark Ascending** arranger cut off in the screenshot ("arr. … by P…") — name him.
- *Lumen* — attribute the composer; "Missa Brevis" alone is not an attribution.
- Sung texts + Polish glosses will be needed for the concert page (`textGloss`), as for every
  other page in the cycle.

## §3 Why this order is the Canticle — the spine for both copy and poster

The *Canticle of the Sun* is a **litany of named creatures**, each introduced by the same refrain,
*Laudato si', mi' Signore*: Brother Sun, Sister Moon and the stars, Brother Wind and the air,
Sister Water, Brother Fire, our sister Mother Earth "who sustains and governs us and produces
diverse fruits with coloured flowers and grass", then those who forgive for love and bear
infirmity and tribulation, and last our sister Bodily Death.

The programme reads as that list. This mapping is **my reading — Florent should confirm or
correct it**, but if it holds it is the concert's `programArc` and the poster's structure at once:

| Canticle | Work |
|---|---|
| *Laudato si'* — the praise itself | Gloria II |
| **frate Sole**, "lo qual è iorno et allumini noi" | Lumen |
| **the text itself**, spoken | Laudes creaturarum (Orff) |
| **sora Luna e le stelle** | Stars |
| **frate Vento**, the air — the bird in it | The Lark Ascending |
| **sor'Acqua**, "utile et humile et pretiosa et casta" — here, frozen | Stoi lód na Prośnie |
| **sora nostra Morte corporale** | Dona eis requiem |
| **frate Focu** | *(no work — see §8)* |
| the blessed after death, "in Your most holy will" | Stetit Angelus |
| **sora nostra matre Terra**, "coloriti fiori et herba" | What A Wonderful World |

Two consequences worth stating plainly:

1. **The form of the text and the form of the evening are the same form: a refrain.** The
   Canticle returns to *Laudato si'* before every creature; the concert returns to *Spiegel im
   Spiegel* before every work. Nine returns either way.
2. **The evening ends in colour and in human faces.** *What a Wonderful World* is the Canticle
   rewritten in 20th-century English and its entire lyric is a list of colours — green trees, red
   roses, blue skies, white clouds, the colours of the rainbow, *the faces of people going by*.
   Placed last, after death, it is the resolution Florent describes as "nadzieja wieczności" —
   and it is the festival's human, arriving on the last page.

## §4 Poster — review of the two sketches (06.09.2026)

Ania's own read ("brakuje stworzenia") and Florent's ("może trochę brakuje stworzenia — parę
rzeczy będzie można dodać") are both right, but they understate the problem: it is not an
addition that is missing.

**What works and must survive the next iteration:** the light. Both sketches have a warm,
un-anxious, unbroken light that is exactly the "zachwyt" register, and both are quiet enough to
belong to this cycle. Sketch 1's layout is the better of the two (title high, one clean footer
band, logo left / dateline right, the superscripted minutes `13⁰⁰` — a nice series-consistent
detail).

**What does not work:**

1. **The Canticle is a list of eight creatures and the poster shows one — the only one that
   is not alive.** A sun alone illustrates roughly an eighth of the text.
2. **The text asks for colour by name.** *"produce diversi fructi con coloriti fiori et herba"* —
   coloured flowers and grass. Both sketches are monochrome gold-beige. The one attribute the
   source demands is the one that is absent, and the closing work is a song *about* the colours.
3. **The setting sun is the wrong direction, and it is already taken.** Francis' sun *is* the day;
   a sun going down over water is the vocabulary of endings — and the ensemble has already used a
   hazy sun over a horizon, for *Aeternam*, an epitaph for Gaza. Two posters in one archive with
   the same composition, one of them meaning "requiem", is a collision.
4. **Sketch 2 reads as a monstrance, not as creation.** Disc + vertical shaft of light + a
   four-point flare is eucharistic-adoration iconography (and the flare reads as a lens artifact
   at poster distance). Its layout is also weaker: the top 60% is an empty field, and the footer
   puts three competing clusters on one baseline (date left / VoctEnsemble centre / venue right,
   the last wrapping to two lines) so the eye has no entry point.
5. **The image is decorative, not made of the evening — and that is a break with the cycle.**
   The two strongest posters in the archive are the two where the poster is built out of the
   concert's own material: *Kontemplacja Wcielenia* (a star whose rays **are** the programme) and
   *9 Kart z Księgi Psalmów* (a scroll drawn out of Hebrew psalm text, the nine psalms fanned as
   pages). Those are hand-made marks, and on a festival wall next to twenty other posters they
   survive; a soft golden gradient sky is the single most generic image of 2026 and will not.
6. **No human, at a festival whose subject is the human.** Ania named this herself.
7. **No slots reserved for the festival.** A festival lockup, partner and patron logos, and
   credits always arrive late and always eat the bottom band. Sketch 2 has no room left for them.
   Also: both files end in a white strip below the image — if that is not an export artifact,
   kill it; a poster needs to bleed.

## §5 Poster — three directions

All three keep the light. All three answer "where is creation" without holy-card kitsch
(a St Francis with a dove would be worse than the abstraction we have).

**A. Litania — the poster is the text.** *(the cycle's own move, and my recommendation)*
Build the image out of the Canticle itself, set in the Umbrian original, with the refrain
*Laudato si', mi' Signore* returning **nine** times — the text's form and the programme's form
being the same form. Do not repeat the Wcielenie star: change the geometry to **strata**, lines
of type banded like a landscape, and let the type carry the colour the text asks for — cold
blue-grey at the top (moon, stars, wind), through water, into green and ochre at the foot
(*matre Terra*, coloured flowers and grass). The creatures are present as their **names**, which
is how Francis put them there. Human presence is the voice speaking the litany — and if the Orff
really is for speaking chorus, a poster made of spoken words is the truest possible image of this
evening. Register: 9 Kart — much white paper, sepia and colour, Cormorant.

**B. Człowiek pośród stworzeń.** *(cheapest to reach from the current sketches)*
Keep sketch 1's light and layout; put one **small** human figure low in the frame, inside the
landscape rather than in front of it. The Canticle's grammar is *brother* and *sister* — kinship,
the human **inside** the list, not above it — which is also the festival's thesis. Precedent in
their own archive: the tiny figure on the road in *Aeternam* is what makes that poster work. Trap
to avoid: a silhouette with raised arms is a motivational poster; keep the figure unlit, small,
turned away.

**C. Zielnik.** *(the most creature-rich, the most different from anything in the archive)*
A herbarium plate: pressed plants, a feather, water, a stone, laid out as specimens with the
Canticle's names as handwritten labels in a naturalist's hand — *frate Vento, sor'Acqua, matre
Terra*. Cool, taxonomic, precise: the exact opposite of a holy card, and it delivers colour
straight from the text. It also rhymes with the festival, since a herbarium is the human
classifying creation. Risk: it can tip into "museum poster" — hold it back with the series
typography and a lot of empty paper.

**Before the next iteration, get from the organiser:** logo pack and key visual, the required
credits/patrons, poster format + bleed + deadline, and whether it is print, digital, or both.
Design the footer band around those slots first.

## §8 Decisions log (11.09.2026) — supersedes anything above it

**Settled by the owner, do not re-litigate:**

- **13:30**, not 13:00.
- **Massenet, *Méditation de Thaïs*, is in; Bach is out.** Objections raised and overruled,
  recorded so nobody re-runs the argument: it doubles the soaring-violin gesture the *Lark*
  already owns for 15:50; it adds five more minutes of legato to an evening that has no attack
  anywhere; its late-Romantic idiom sits apart from Pärt/Borkowski/Ešenvalds/Dubra; and it is the
  most-played violin encore there is. Against that: it is the only work in the programme whose
  subject is one interior life turning, which is on-title for the festival, and it plays on the
  forces available. Position in the running order **not yet known**.
- **Pärt goes to the organ, played from the gallery** — Florent's word: *"Pärt z góry będzie
  leciał jako leitmotiv"*. So the returning element is spatially separated from everything else,
  by design, not as a workaround. There is **no piano and no percussion** in this concert. Open:
  whether Radu is in the gallery too (the piece is a duo) — this decides whether the violin is
  the evening's continuous voice or a single guest appearance. Craft note: the piano part in
  *Spiegel* decays and the organ does not, so the silences are Florent's to build with
  registration and release.
- **Logos/credits**: deferred, likely a strip added at the foot late.

**Corrections to §3's mapping** (the version above is now right, this says why):

- *Stetit Angelus* is **not** the fire strophe — I claimed the censer carried it and that was a
  stretch. Revelation 8:3–4 is about the smoke of prayer rising, and Francis' fire is *"piękny i
  radosny, i silny, i mocny"*. Placed second-to-last, after the requiem, the work reads instead
  as the second half of the death strophe: *"Błogosławieni, którzy znajdą się w Twej najświętszej
  woli."*
- So **two** strophes have no work of their own: **fire**, and **those who forgive**. Neither is
  absent from the evening — the Orff carries the whole poem, including both. Florent's ruling on
  the map as a whole: *"muzyka ma być swego rodzaju osobistym komentarzem, ale nie musi odhaczyć
  absolutnie każdy punkt."* Treat the mapping as a reading, never as a checklist to fill.
- The first five items **do** run in the Canticle's own order (invocation, sun, moon/stars, air,
  water) — suggestive, but any sky-to-earth programme might land there, so it is not proof the
  order was designed.
- Standing observation, independent of the mapping and still unanswered: with no piano, no
  percussion and the organ sustaining, **the evening has no percussive attack anywhere**. The
  only consonants in roughly an hour of music are the Orff, if it is performed as a speaking
  chorus. Florent wants "radość i światło" — it has to come from somewhere.

**Poster** (Ania, 2nd iteration — the direction was taken):

The sketch is now built from the programme, like *Wcielenie* and *9 Kart*: work titles set in a
ring around Michelangelo's Creation-of-Adam hands, light coming from between them, title in the
lower third. The sunset/*Aeternam* collision is gone. Two open notes sent back: **write *Spiegel
im Spiegel* nine times around the ring**, alternating with the works, so the poster shows the
refrain that is the evening's actual form (and gives the circle a reading entry); and **"św.
Franciszek z Asyżu" currently sits in the composer slot**, so it parses as "św. Franciszek z
Asyżu — R. Dubra". One question left with Florent: the Sistine hands are God reaching down to
Adam, while the Canticle's posture is horizontal kinship (*brother* sun, *sister* water) — good
for the festival's Teilhardian title, less good for Francis. Proposed fix that keeps all of
Ania's work: make them **two human hands**. Of the two light treatments, the calmer one is
better — in the other the fingers nearly touch and burst into a star, losing the gap that is the
whole point of the original, and the rays cut through the titles.

**Load-bearing consequences:**

- **Pärt is not a work in the programme and not a refrain of "Laudato si'".** It is the *mirror*:
  Gen 1:27, creation reflecting its Creator. Hence the count of episodes has never mattered to
  Florent and has already changed once — do not build anything (poster, copy) on how many times
  it returns. The two mirrors **meet** at the end, which is why the last Pärt episode stops
  immediately before *Stetit Angelus*. A playlist ending "09 [zakończenie]" at position 18 is
  therefore correct, not a slip.
- **The sun focus is deliberate and defended**: the Polish reception title is *Pieśń słoneczna*,
  so the sun is not one creature among eight here, it is the title. Florent's rule for the whole
  project: *"Za dużo wątków nie zmieścimy."* Proposals to add creature imagery, a second ring of
  creature names, or a water register to the poster were **withdrawn on this basis**.
- **The Sistine hands are justified by the concept**, not in spite of it. The objection that they
  assert a vertical God→Adam theology against Francis' horizontal kinship is withdrawn: Florent's
  thesis *is* the vertical relation, with the creatures as the road to it. Better still, the two
  hands facing each other with a gap **are** the two mirrors — the poster states Gen 1:27 and
  Pärt's title with one image. Ania arrived at it before the concept was written down.

## §12 The page became an invitation (14.09.2026, same day, second pass)

**This supersedes §11's page and §11's account of the notes.** §11 shipped the Polish page inside the
template that describes a sung evening, and reading it back the developer named what was wrong:
*„weszliśmy w schemat opisujący co się wydarzyło."* Four faults, of which only one was the layout.

### What was actually wrong

1. **The page did not print the invitation the corpus already held.** `essence` reached only the
   `<meta description>` and the JSON-LD; `invitation` — the one paragraph written for a reader who has
   not decided to come — was read by the landing's band and by nothing else. `admission: free`,
   `festival` and `address` were **JSON-LD only**, so a search engine was told this is a free concert
   inside a named festival at a given address while the reader was told none of it.
2. **The reflection was misattributed *and* redundant** (below).
3. **The notes reinstated a mapping the concept had rejected** (below).
4. **The order was a document's order** — the poster at the foot as a keepsake, and front matter for
   a document with three rows.

### The mode, not a new page

The route was never the problem. A bespoke page for this one evening was considered and rejected: it
abandons the 44 drafted EN/FR keys under `concert.pochwala-stworzenia.*`, plus `lib/eventSchema`, the
cycle numeral, the prev/next walk, the tabula, the share card and the reveal register — or duplicates
all of them. `landing/16-proximum.css` already carries a note naming "restating `.station--ahead`" as
the one hazard that sheet carries, and a bespoke announcement surface would have made three
restatements of one form. Every future announced evening differs (a festival, a grant, a concert
abroad), so five of them would have been five forks of a 1948-line file. **Variance belongs in the
corpus as optional fields; it does not belong in new files.**

`ConcertPage.astro` therefore names one fact — `unsung`, read off `upcomingStation` — with five
consequences: the door band is raised, the tabula is held back, the notice list is offered once (and
the coda's rail drops its own door so the page does not ask twice), the poster moves up into the door
band, and the encore stays sealed. `ahead` was already suppressed, so the page never announces
itself. **The first deploy after 11.10 turns the invitation into a record with nobody editing
anything.**

New corpus fields, all optional: `endTime`, `mapUrl`, `venueNote`, `festivalNote`,
`program[].locus`. Each is classified in `copydesk/contract.mjs` — the test that catches an
unclassified path is the one that caught `festival.*` and `posterCredit` before, and it is green at
29/29.

**One thing in the plan was wrong and was reverted mid-pass.** The reflection and the programme were
to be swapped while the evening is ahead, on the argument that an invitation says what will be heard
before it says what it means. Implemented with flex `order`, which moves what the eye meets and
leaves the document where it was: a screen reader and the tab key would have taken the two bands in
one sequence while everybody else read them in the other. And the DOM order turned out to be the
better reading anyway — the próg ends on the angel censing the altar with the prayers of all the
saints, and `reflectionNote` is what that sentence rhymes with. The two are one thought, and a
nine-hundred-line programme book between them weakens both. **A page that wants a different order has
to be written in that order.**

### The reflection is gone, and must not come back

`reflection` and `reflectionAttribution` are deleted. The account was **generated, not written** — he
said so when he sent it (§9) — so it could not carry his name, against
`concert-detail-pages-spec.md` §0's locked "never invent a quote and attribute it to a real person".
It was also the third statement of a thesis `essence` and `programArc` already make better, and it
was measurably the prose the copy canon exists to remove: „nie są więc tylko… ale prowadzą" spends
the site's single apophasis budget, and „i właśnie tutaj domyka się cała droga koncertu" explains its
own metaphor in the next sentence. The band survives carrying only `reflectionNote` — ours, unsigned,
the All Saints rhyme. **Florent is on the page as its conductor. A signed paragraph belongs there
only when he writes one.** And then he wrote one, of a different kind — see the arc below.

### `note` / `locus`: one field was doing two jobs

§11 claimed the four works lifted from `wolanie-gor` were "given new tails". **That is not true of
the Lark**, whose note is that evening's word for word with nothing added; `Stoi lód` is verbatim
plus one sentence; `Stars` is two of three sentences verbatim.

The copying was not the fault. The tails were: **four of the ten notes ended on the same move,** „W
Kantyku to strofa o…" (Lumen, Stars, Stoi lód, Dona eis requiem) — the creature-map as a
**checklist**, which §3 says it must never be and which §9 records Florent withdrawing from the
poster on the grounds „Za dużo wątków nie zmieścimy". Mechanical, too: four works got a stanza and
six did not, so a reader could not tell structure from editorial habit.

The cause was structural. `note` was being asked to say both what a work **is** — provenance,
scoring, whose words it sets, facts identical in any programme — and what it **does here**. A record
can merge those because its reader already knows the evening; an invitation cannot.

So the field is split:

- **`note` = the fact.** It may be identical across two concerts, because the fact is: Teasdale wrote
  the poem once, Ešenvalds scored the glasses once, Sykulski's commission happened once. Already
  vetted in three languages, and a translation accepted for the other evening is the right answer
  here rather than a fresh paraphrase of it.
- **`locus` = the reading.** One clause, this evening's descent, and **the only part rewritten when a
  work returns in another programme.**

**The audit rule, applied to all ten: anything in `note` that is a reading moves to `locus` or goes.**
`Stars` lost „Harmonie są otwarte i przestronne; to najwyższy punkt wieczoru" — that was
`wolanie-gor`'s shape, not this one's. **`Lumen` lost its note entirely**, because both its sentences
were readings and the booklet is silent on the rest; the spec's answer to that is an empty field, and
the Canticle's line about brother sun is printed under the Orff anyway. `Ménétrier` lost the em-dash
its inserted clause had introduced. The ten `locus` clauses come out of `programArc`, which was
already making promises the notes never kept — „skowronek, który wznosi się, aż ginie w świetle" is
the Lark's, and the Lark was the note with no tail at all.

**The Canticle is now cited three times in three different moves**, not four times in one shape: the
Orff carries the whole text, `Stoi lód` turns one stanza over (frozen water against „pożyteczna i
pokorna, i drogocenna, i czysta"), `Dona eis requiem` is the floor the Canticle greets by name. A
citation hung on work after work in the same shape stops being a reading.

**The tense rule, which the split makes load-bearing:** no field that survives the flip may be
phrased so the flip falsifies it, and `locus` is where that bites hardest. The corpus already obeys
it — `prologue`, `programArc`, `essence`, `invitation` and `reflectionNote` are present-tense and true
on both sides of 11 October.

### The programme band inverted

Ten five-sentence paragraphs of provenance buried the arc that had just promised the evening. Now
`locus` stays open and the `note` opens **with** the sung text, in one box per work where the page had
one for the text alone — fewer disclosures than before, and `<details>` content is in the HTML, so
nothing is lost to search. The cue names the largest thing inside: `textCue` where there is a sung
text, the new `workCue` („O utworze") where there is not. On this evening that is 4 and 5; the tenth
work has neither and opens nothing.
